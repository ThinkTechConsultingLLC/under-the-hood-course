# M1 — What a computer really is

> You use one every day, but the box itself is kind of a mystery. By the end of today it won't
> be — we'll open the hood (no wires, no risk) and you'll not only name the parts, you'll read
> your own computer's specs like a pro and understand *why the same kind of chip that draws your
> games is what trains AI*.

**Today's win:** you can read your own computer's specs — processor (cores + GHz), memory, storage (and whether it's an SSD), and graphics (GPU) — say what each part does, and explain why AI runs on GPUs.

### Today you will
- Find and *read* your own machine's specs — including **cores**, **clock speed**, and **SSD vs HDD**
- Match each part (CPU, RAM, storage, GPU) to its real job
- Understand **why AI needs GPUs** — and that a phone, laptop, and data center are the same parts, scaled

### If you get stuck
- Totally normal — and nothing in this lab can harm your computer. We're only *looking*.
- Re-read the **✅ You should now see** line under the step. Screens differ by computer — compare with your breakout partner.
- Numbers look weird or missing? Note it and move on; your instructor will help.

### Optional challenge
Look up the specs of your **phone** and a **cloud GPU** (search "NVIDIA H100"). Same parts — wildly different scale. How much more powerful is the data-center chip? Bring a number to the chat.
